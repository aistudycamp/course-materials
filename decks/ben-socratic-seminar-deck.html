The bake-off

One prompt, three models.

Haiku 4.5
Fast. Barely thinks. Good for quick, simple work.
Sonnet 5
The everyday pick. Balances speed and depth.
Opus 5
Slow. Thinks it through. Good for hard, ambiguous problems.
Why outputs differ

It never picks one fixed answer.

Same model,
twice
It predicts a word, then the next, then the next. Each step has more than one good option, so a second run can take a different path.
Different
models
Different training means different instincts for what a good next word looks like. That gap is much bigger than the first one.
Fast versus slow

Cheap does not mean worse.

Why Haiku is fast
Less thinking per answer. Built for quick turnaround, not for working through something hard.
When fast wins
Simple, well-defined, high-volume work. Same result, a fraction of the wait.
The effort dial

Effort is how long it thinks before answering.

Low effort
Answers on the first idea that looks right. Fine when the task has one clear path.
High effort
Weighs options, checks its own work, catches mistakes before committing. Costs time.
On an easy task, high effort mostly buys a slower wait for the same answer.
Why the code differs

Models do not agree on how much is enough.

More code
More scaffolding, comments, and defensive checks. Can mean more thorough.
Less code
Terser by default. Can mean tighter, or can mean it skipped something.
More is not automatically better. Check what it built, not how much it wrote.

The rule

Pick the model for the task. Step up when the cheap one struggles.

Not sure which to use?
Start with the one that fits most of your work. Move up only when it struggles.
