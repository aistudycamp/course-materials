3:00 · 3 min Our own recovery guide opens by telling you "almost nothing is truly lost." That is true, and it is true about your code. Your database has no commits. Any service you store data in

Three questions

01

Does it back up at all?

Not "is it reliable." Does a copy exist from before today.

02

On my plan?

Backups are usually a paid feature. Free tiers often have none.

03

How do I restore?

A button, a support ticket, or nothing. Find out before you need it.

Instant, free
No backups at all
Instant, Pro $30/mo
Daily, kept 7 days
6:00 · 2.5 min These three questions work on anything you store data in, now and in five years. Ask them the day you pick a service, not the day you need them. Most people never ask question one and assume the answer is yes. Our worked example: everyone here is on Instant free, which has no backups at all. Not a shorter window, none. Pro is thirty dollars a month for a daily backup kept seven days. So the real question is never "should I be more careful," it is "is what is in here worth thirty dollars a month yet." Every service answers these differently. You have to go look.

11:30 · 2.5 min You already know this shape from the API keys guide: same variable names, different places. Local on your computer, production on your host. This is one more variable. The thing your laptop connects to should not be the thing your users are typing into. Same idea whether it is Instant, Supabase, or a Sheet. On proportion: for a homework project one environment is fine and I am not telling you to run two for a sticker board. The line is the moment there is data in there you did not type yourself. A real user, a client, anything you would have to apologize for losing. Cross it and you do both on the same day: split the environment, pay for backups.
